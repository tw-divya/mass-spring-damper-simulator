Model

Newton's second law, Hooke's law (F = −kx, k in N/m) and viscous damping (F = −c·dx/dt, c in N·s/m) give m x″ + c x′ + k x = F(t) with m in kg, x in m, F in N. It is a linear second-order ODE with constant coefficients; its solution is the complementary function (free vibration) plus a particular integral (forced response).

Laplace method

Using L{x″} = s²X − s x(0) − x′(0) and L{x′} = sX − x(0):

X(s) = [F(s) + m s x(0) + m x′(0) + c x(0)] / (m s² + c s + k)

With zero initial conditions, G(s) = 1/(m s² + c s + k) is the transfer function. The characteristic equation m s² + c s + k = 0 has roots s = −ζωn ± ωn√(ζ²−1). Inputs: step F0/s, impulse J, sine F0Ω/(s²+Ω²). Partial fractions and the inverse transform give x(t).

Damping cases

ωn = √(k/m) (rad/s), cc = 2√(mk) (N·s/m), ζ = c/cc.
Underdamped (0<ζ<1): complex roots, decaying oscillation at ωd = ωn√(1−ζ²). Critically damped (ζ=1): repeated real root, fastest non-oscillatory return. Overdamped (ζ>1): two negative real roots, slow return. Undamped (ζ=0): sustained oscillation at ωn.

Numerical method and impulse

The ODE becomes x′ = v, v′ = (F − cv − kx)/m and is integrated with fourth-order Runge-Kutta. An ideal impulse Jδ(t−t0) is not an enormous finite force: integrating the ODE across t0 gives a velocity jump v(t0⁺) = v(t0⁻) + J/m, applied directly by the solver. The "Analytical" curve is the exact inverse-Laplace solution; the lab reports the maximum difference. Results are computed, not laboratory measurements.

Applications

Vehicle suspensions and shock absorbers, vibration isolation, buildings and seismic response, instrument damping (e.g. galvanometers, accelerometers), control-system modelling, and the electrical analogy: mass ↔ inductance L, damper ↔ resistance R, spring ↔ 1/C in a series RLC circuit.
